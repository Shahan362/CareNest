CareNest Use Cases

Three kinds of users share one system: the agency admin, caregivers and patients. Each role has its own screens, six features are common to everyone, and the dashed purple arrows mark optional steps that extend a main task. Pick a role to highlight only what that person can do.

Highlight:

How the features connect

The life of one visit

How work done by one role is meant to reach the others.

  1. 1
    The agency and its people join

    The admin registers the agency on the sign-up page (/signup?role=admin). Caregivers and patients sign up with the agency code (/signup?role=caregiver, /signup?role=patient), or the admin adds them directly (/admin/caregivers, /admin/patients).

  2. 2
    A care request reaches the admin

    The patient asks for a visit or a change on the Requests page (/patient/requests) and can follow its status there.

  3. 3
    The admin assigns the visit to a caregiver

    On the Scheduler (/admin/scheduler) the admin picks a caregiver, optionally using caregiver suggestions and the workload heatmap. The visit then appears in the caregiver's schedule (/caregiver/schedule) and in the patient's visits (/patient/visits).

  4. 4
    The caregiver records what happened

    During the visit the caregiver ticks off the checklist, marks the visit complete and writes notes (/caregiver/checklist, /caregiver/notes).

  5. 5
    Feedback and billing close the loop

    The patient rates the visit (/patient/feedback). The admin sees results in Reports (/admin/reports) and bills the completed visit (/admin/billing).

In the current code, these hand-offs are designed but not wired up

Every page loads its own built-in sample data and there is no database, so sign-up and login don't create or check real accounts, and a visit the admin schedules doesn't actually appear on the caregiver's page. The diagram shows how the app is meant to work. Connecting a shared database is what makes steps 1 to 5 real.
